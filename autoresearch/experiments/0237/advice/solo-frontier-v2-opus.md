## 4. Pair: minimum controls

**Precondition:** pair has no validated target. Of the two failures in evidence, one is lifecycle and one rests on an unsupported row. Only solo failures that are requirement-valid and lifecycle-clean on the corrected instrument license pair.

- **Identical conditions.** Use the same automatic trigger, the same frozen snapshot at the trigger point, a byte-identical check request, and a read-only reviewer so only the owner edits. H and P go through the same awaited launcher.
- **Credit only from adjacent contrasts.**
  - S over B is the value of the check.
  - H over S is a fresh awaited session.
  - P over H is the other model, and it is the only contrast where the launcher and its await cancel.
  - S runs the check in the owner's own turn, so that check cannot be lost to child lifetime. A gain over B that S also shows is lifecycle or the check, not peer reasoning.
- **Credit only for flips.** Grade the trigger-point snapshot offline in every arm. Credit is rows failing there and passing at the end, traceable to a finding the owner had not already recorded. A run already correct at the snapshot earns zero credit and full cost.
- **Trigger as its own instrument.** Log fire/no-fire in B without acting. It must fire on residual-failure cells and stay silent on easy controls; always-on is mandatory review by another name.
- **Accounting.** Everything is charged per correct delivery, per owner-engine and peer-engine. Peer usage comes from the peer's own native record and is unknown if missing.

Run it as a ladder, S then H then P, and stop at the first rung that clears the residuals.

**Strongest counterargument:** S and H are arms with no product value, and flip accounting undercounts diffuse peer value the oracle cannot see. H is still the one control you cannot drop: without it, P over B cannot separate another model's reasoning from an awaited second pass.
